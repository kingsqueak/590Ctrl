590Ctrl Help

590Ctrl controls a Kenwood TS-590SG (or TS-590S) over its USB port. Hover over any control for a short description of it.

Connecting the radio

Connect the radio's USB-B port to the computer. That one cable carries CAT control (a serial port) and the radio's audio (a sound device named "USB Audio CODEC").

  • Port and speed: open the Setup tab, or click the radio's name in the top line. 590Ctrl picks the radio's port by itself; ⟳ looks for ports again. The speed must match radio menu 068 (USB port speed), 115200 bps from the factory.
  • Connect: press Connect. The top line shows the model and firmware once the radio answers.
  • Power: the power symbol at the top left turns the radio on and off. If the radio is off, pressing it wakes the radio and connects.
  • No radio: choose Simulator (no radio) as the port to try the app.
  • RTS: the radio only talks while RTS is asserted, so 590Ctrl holds it. If RTS is assigned to PTT in the radio's menus, connecting keys the transmitter.

While connected, changes made on the radio's front panel show in the app. 590Ctrl always stops transmitting before it disconnects or quits.

The window

The operating controls are always in view: the VFOs, the meter, the band and mode buttons, and the Receive, Filter and Transmit panels. The CW, QRZ, Equalizer, Menu and Setup tabs hold the rest.

  • View (top right) moves the tabs below the controls or beside them, in a wider and shorter window. View → Tabs Below and Tabs Beside do the same. The window's size is remembered for each.
  • Tabs hides the tabs and leaves a small operating window.
  • Mini shrinks the app to the mini window.
  • LOCK and FINE are the radio's LOCK and FINE keys.
  • Top line: the radio's connection, and beside it the TCI server while it's on. Click either to reach its settings. A note appears here when a newer version of 590Ctrl is out.

Tuning and modes

  • Tune: scroll over any digit of VFO A or B to change that digit, one step for each click of the wheel. Double-click a VFO to type a frequency.
  • A / B: choose the VFO to receive and transmit on.
  • SPLIT: transmit on the other VFO.
  • A=B: copy the receive VFO's frequency and mode to the other VFO.
  • RIT / XIT: shift the receive or transmit frequency by the offset beside them. Turn one on to adjust the offset; CLR sets it to 0.
  • Band buttons: 160 m to 6 m, and GEN for general coverage. Click the current band again to step through its band memories.
  • Mode buttons: LSB, USB, CW, CW-R, FSK, FSK-R, FM and AM. DATA turns data mode on in LSB, USB, FM and AM.
  • TX lamp beside the VFOs lights while the radio transmits.

Meter and antennas

  • Receiving: signal strength from S1 to S9 +60 dB with peak hold. The yellow marker shows the RF gain setting.
  • SQL shows in green over the reading while the squelch is set and open.
  • Transmitting: power in watts, with SWR, speech processor compression (COMP) and ALC below.
  • ANT 1 / 2: the antenna connector in use.
  • RX ANT: receive on the RX ANT input.
  • DRV: the drive output (or antenna output, as set in menu 085).
  • AT: puts the internal antenna tuner in line. It does not start a tune.
  • AT TUNE: runs a tuner cycle. The radio transmits while it tunes; click again to stop.

Receive

  • AF: speaker volume. MUTE silences the speaker and the USB audio going to the computer; it's red while on, and the levels come back when you unmute.
  • RF: RF gain. ATT steps through attenuator, preamp and off.
  • SQL: squelch.
  • AGC: steps through off, slow and fast (not in FM). The slider is the time constant for the mode in use.
  • NB: noise blanker, stepping through off, NB1, NB2 and both. The slider is the level of the one that's on (not adjustable with both).
  • NR: noise reduction, stepping through off, NR1 and NR2 (no NR2 in FM). The slider is NR1's level or NR2's follow speed.

Filter and notch

  • A / B: the radio's two IF filter settings.
  • High cut and Low cut, or Width and Shift: the filter's edges. Which pair shows depends on the mode, as on the radio.
  • BC: beat cancel, stepping through off, BC1 and BC2. BC1 removes a steady beat, BC2 several or keyed ones. Not in CW or FSK.
  • AUTO: auto notch, which finds and removes a carrier.
  • MANUAL: a notch at the Position you set. WIDE makes it wider.

Transmit

  • Power: 5 to 100 W (5 to 25 W in AM).
  • Mic: microphone gain for SSB and AM.
  • PROC: the speech processor. Click to turn it on; click again to set its input level with the slider, again for its output level, and again to turn it off.
  • VOX: voice-operated transmit, with its gain. In CW the same key is break-in.
  • MON: hear your own transmit audio, with its level.
  • SEND: transmits until you click it again.
  • TX TUNE: transmits a carrier at the tune power (Setup tab) until you click it again.
  • TX: transmits while you hold it down.

Tune power, carrier level, VOX delay and the transmit audio filter are on the Setup tab.

CW tab

Keyer

  • Speed: the keyer speed, 4 to 60 WPM. Keyboard CW is sent at this speed too.
  • Pitch: the CW pitch and sidetone, 300 to 1000 Hz.
  • BREAK-IN: keying transmits. The slider is how long the radio stays in transmit after the last element; all the way left is FULL break-in (QSK).
  • CW TUNE: the radio zeroes in on the CW signal being received. CW and CW-R only.

Tuning display

The display under CW TUNE shows the audio being received from 100 to 1300 Hz. Each signal is a peak. The red line marks the CW pitch and moves with the Pitch slider.

  • To zero beat a station: tune until its peak sits on the red line. Your transmitted signal is then on its frequency.
  • When it runs: while the CW tab is showing and the radio is in CW or CW-R.
  • Audio: it listens to the radio's USB audio, the same as TCI and DeepCW. See If something doesn't work if it stays empty.

Keyboard CW

Type in the line under the decoder and press Enter or SEND. The radio keys the text, and transmits it if break-in is on. Long text is fed to the radio as it goes. STOP stops sending and drops what hasn't been keyed.

Letters, digits and . , / ? = : ' " ( ) * + - @ can be sent. Prosigns:

Decoder

DECODE prints the Morse being received, in CW and CW-R. There are two decoders:

  • Radio: the TS-590SG's own decoder. It prints at once and is best on clean signals. Threshold sets how strong a signal must be: raise it if noise prints as characters.
  • DeepCW: a neural network that listens to the radio's USB audio. It copies weak, fading and hand-sent CW far better, 2 to 4 seconds behind the signal. It needs a pitch of 400 Hz or more. The note beside the buttons shows what it's doing.
  • WORDS: puts back the word spaces a decoder misses, so CQSSK1XMK1XM reads CQ SS K1XM K1XM. It works with either decoder.
  • Clear empties the text.

DeepCW is in the macOS app on Apple silicon Macs. Where it isn't available the Radio and DeepCW choice doesn't appear. The TS-590S has no decoder of its own, so on it DeepCW is the only one.

QRZ tab

  • Account…: your QRZ username and password, your station callsign, and your Logbook API key (QRZ Logbook → Settings → API). The key is only needed for logging. A remembered password is kept unencrypted in the app's settings.
  • Look Up: type a callsign and press Enter. Every field QRZ returns is listed, with the operator's photo. Double-click the callsign, web page, email or image to open it. Without a QRZ subscription, QRZ returns only part of each record.
  • Worked before: with a Logbook API key, a call you've worked turns green, with the date, time, band and mode of the last QSO and how many you've logged.
  • Log Contact: adds the QSO to your QRZ Logbook. The frequency, band, mode and power come from the radio and the time is the current UTC time. Set the RST and a comment beside it. Logging through the API needs a QRZ subscription.
  • Mode to log: follows the radio. In SSB-DATA it's a guess (PSK31), so pick FT8, FT4, RTTY or whatever you're using.

Equalizer tab

The RX Equalizer and TX Equalizer each have 18 bands.

  • Curve: the radio's EQ curve for the current kind of mode (SSB, SSB-DATA, CW, FM, FM-DATA, AM, AM-DATA or FSK). U is the User curve, the one whose band levels the radio keeps.
  • Sliders: the level of each band. They can't be changed while the curve is OFF.
  • Flat sets every band to 0 dB. Reload reads the curve back from the radio.
  • Presets: Save As… stores the levels under a name, Apply writes the chosen preset to the radio, Delete removes it, and More imports and exports presets as files.
  • Auto-load into U: the radio has one User curve for receive and one for transmit, shared by every mode. Link a preset to the current kind of mode here, and 590Ctrl writes it into the User curve each time you enter that mode, so each mode gets its own curve.

The transmit equalizer is always off in CW and FSK.

Menu tab

Every setting in the radio's menu, for both of its menu sets, Menu A and Menu B, side by side. Both are read the first time you open the tab, which takes a few seconds.

  • Change a setting: pick from the list, or type the number or text, in the A or B column. Rows where A and B differ are in bold.
  • Menu in use: A / B switches the radio between its two sets. Every menu setting changes with it.
  • Copy A → B and Copy B → A write one set into the other, after showing how many settings will change.
  • Filter by number or name: type a menu number or part of a name to find a setting.
  • Reload reads both sets again.

PF and key assignments are function numbers from the radio's instruction manual (255 is OFF). The COM and USB port speeds are shown but can't be changed here, since that would drop the connection.

Setup tab

  • Radio connection: the port, the speed and Connect. See Connecting the radio.
  • Programs sharing the radio (TCI): Settings… opens the TCI server settings.
  • Computer audio: the USB audio levels. USB in (menu 071) is audio from the computer to the transmitter; USB out (menu 072) is received audio to the computer.
  • Transmit set-up: Tune power for TX TUNE, Carrier level for CW, FSK and AM, and VOX delay.
  • Transmit audio filter: the low and high cut of the transmit audio, for SSB and AM and for SSB-DATA.

Sharing the radio (TCI)

TCI lets programs such as WSJT-X, fldigi and TCIdigi use the radio while 590Ctrl is connected to it, so they share it. Open Tools → TCI Server…, or Settings… on the Setup tab.

  • Let TCI programs control the radio turns the server on.
  • Address: 127.0.0.1 keeps it to this computer. 0.0.0.0 also accepts programs on other computers on your network. TCI has no password, so anyone who can reach it can tune and key the radio.
  • Port: 40001 unless you change it.
  • Share the radio's USB audio: sends received audio to the programs and plays their transmit audio into the radio, so they need no sound device of their own. Leave it off if the program uses the USB Audio CODEC itself.
  • Receive audio from / Transmit audio to: the sound devices to use, normally the radio's.

In the program, choose TCI and enter the same address and port. In WSJT-X: Settings → Radio, rig TCI Client RX1, PTT method CAT; and under Settings → Audio choose TCI audio for both to use the shared audio.

590Ctrl must be connected to the radio; until it is, the programs are told the radio is off. A program that disconnects while transmitting unkeys the radio.

Mini window

Mini at the top right, or View → Mini Window, swaps the app for a small window to park in a corner of the screen: VFO A and B, A, B and A=B, the meter, MUT and a large hold-to-transmit TX.

  • Drag its edge to resize it; everything scales with it.
  • It stays on top of other apps. 📌 turns that off.
  • ⤢, or closing it, brings back the full window.

Monitor, appearance and updates

  • View → CAT Monitor shows the commands going to and from the radio and TCI programs. Hide meter polling leaves out the constant meter readings.
  • View → Appearance: System, Light or Dark.
  • Updates: at start 590Ctrl asks GitHub whether there's a newer release and shows a note in the top line if so. Click the note to open the download page; ✕ hides it until the next version. Check for Updates… asks now. Nothing is downloaded or installed for you.

If something doesn't work

  • It won't connect: check the speed against radio menu 068. On macOS choose cu.SLAB_USBtoUART. On Linux your account must be in the dialout group (uucp on Arch); log in again after adding it. On Windows the radio needs the Silicon Labs CP210x driver.
  • The tuning display or DeepCW shows nothing on macOS: they hear the radio through what macOS calls a microphone. Allow 590Ctrl in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone, then leave the CW tab and come back, or press DECODE again.
  • "The radio's USB audio device wasn't found": the computer doesn't see the "USB Audio CODEC". Check the USB cable, or choose the devices in the TCI settings.
  • The tuning display is flat: raise USB out under Computer audio on the Setup tab, and check MUTE is off.
  • DeepCW prints nothing: set the pitch to 400 Hz or more, and give it a few seconds.
  • A TCI program says the radio is off: connect 590Ctrl to the radio first.
  • A control is greyed out: the radio doesn't offer it in the current mode, or isn't connected.
